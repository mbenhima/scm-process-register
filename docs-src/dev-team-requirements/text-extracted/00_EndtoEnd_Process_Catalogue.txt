Executive Summary

This catalogue is the single reference for every End-to-End (E2E)
process that composes journi's ten macro processes (D01) into complete,
cross-cutting business flows. Four E2E processes are formally registered
today in D32h, the End-to-End Process Registry; four additional
processes are proposed here as extensions, each grounded in a
cross-reference already written into the affected macro processes' own
descriptions rather than invented independently; and eight further
processes are proposed as transformation-type-specific extensions, one
for each of the project archetypes defined in the journi Functional
Specification v1.3 and the Transformation Types User Guide. Every macro
process (MP-01 through MP-10) appears in at least one E2E chain — no
macro process is orphaned from an end-to-end narrative.

Functional Requirement Coverage

Collectively, the 16 E2E processes exercise every functional requirement
domain defined in D13 (Application Requirements):

-   Stakeholder & Impact, Sponsorship, and Communication domains
    (REQ-001, REQ-002, REQ-014) — exercised by E2E-01.

-   ADKAR Engine, Training, and Champion Network domains (REQ-002,
    REQ-016) — exercised by E2E-02, and its signal-consolidation
    dependency made explicit by E2E-05.

-   Resistance Management and Divergence Detection domains (REQ-003,
    REQ-004) — exercised by E2E-03, with the champion-to-resistance
    hand-off made explicit by E2E-07.

-   Sustainment and Reinforcement domains (REQ-005) — exercised by
    E2E-04, with the governance-escalation thread made explicit by
    E2E-08.

-   WBS, Phase Gate, and Joint Decision domains (REQ-011, REQ-016,
    REQ-017, REQ-018) — exercised directly by E2E-06, the only chain
    that bridges the change-management and project-management sides of
    the platform.

-   AI Governance domain (REQ-006, REQ-007, REQ-008) — exercised
    indirectly across every chain that includes an AI-assisted task (see
    each process's Process Flow table for AI Task rows).

-   Training & Skills, QHSE Certification, and Cultural/Behavioral
    domains — exercised by the eight transformation-type-specific chains
    (E2E-ERP through E2E-TSD), each composed from the same ten macro
    processes.

No functional requirement domain in D13 is left unexercised by at least
one of the 16 chains above.

E2E-01 — Readiness & Mobilization

  Status: Registered in D32h

Detailed Description

The mobilization chain carries an initiative from an approved change
scope through to a fully mobilized organization: the stakeholder impact
assessment (MP-01) identifies who is affected and how; sponsorship and
governance (MP-02) secures the executive backing and decision cadence
the initiative needs; and communication and awareness (MP-03) ensures
the affected population actually hears — and understands — what is
changing and why. This is the platform's front door: almost every later
process assumes E2E-01 has already produced a confirmed stakeholder map
and an active sponsor.

Trigger: Change scope statement approved

Terminal State: Stakeholder map published, sponsor coalition active,
communication cadence running

E2E-02 — Capability Build

  Status: Registered in D32h

Detailed Description

The capability chain turns a mobilized organization into a capable one.
Training and capability enablement (MP-05) builds Knowledge and Ability
against the future-state process; the champion network (MP-06) extends
the Change Manager's reach onto the floor and surfaces early sentiment
signals; and readiness diagnostics (MP-07) continuously measures whether
the organization is actually becoming ready, not just attending
sessions. The three run largely in parallel rather than strictly
sequentially — training completion and champion coverage both feed the
same readiness signal MP-07 consolidates.

Trigger: Persona segments confirmed from E2E-01

Terminal State: Cohorts trained, champion network active, readiness
signal flowing

E2E-03 — Resistance-to-Commitment

  Status: Registered in D32h

Detailed Description

The recovery chain is triggered reactively — by a logged barrier or a
detected divergence pattern — rather than running on a fixed schedule.
Resistance and barrier management (MP-04) classifies and responds to a
named barrier; divergence and risk detection (MP-08) catches the
harder-to-see case where a person or cohort is technically capable but
not yet emotionally transitioned; and hypercare and floor coaching
(MP-09) provides the sustained, in-context support that converts either
case into genuine commitment. This chain can fire many times across a
single initiative's lifecycle, each time closing with a re-score rather
than a calendar date.

Trigger: A barrier or divergence pattern is first logged

Terminal State: Cohort re-scored above threshold or formally closed as
resolved

E2E-04 — Sustainment & Closure

  Status: Registered in D32h

Detailed Description

The closure chain is the only E2E process built from a single macro
process rather than a multi-process composition — it is registered as
its own E2E entry because its SIPOC is materially different (a closing
initiative rather than an active one) from every other chain.
Reinforcement and sustainment management (MP-10) runs the 60/90-day
audit cadence, confirms Lewin Refreeze from evidence rather than the
calendar, and formally hands the initiative off to Business-as-Usual
ownership.

Trigger: 60-day checkpoint reached

Terminal State: Project formally closed, Refreeze confirmed, lessons
logged

E2E-05 — Signal Aggregation Loop

  Status: Proposed extension (not yet in D32h)

Detailed Description

This chain makes explicit a data dependency already written into three
macro processes' own descriptions: MP-03 states it "feeds Awareness
scoring back into the ADKAR Engine," and MP-05 states it "feeds
Knowledge/Ability scores back into the ADKAR Engine for divergence
cross-checking." Both signals land in MP-07's Composite Readiness Index,
which MP-08's Divergence Pattern Detector then evaluates. Today this
flow is split silently across three separate registered E2E chains
(E2E-01, E2E-02, E2E-03) with no single artifact describing the loop end
to end.

Trigger: Awareness (MP-03) and Knowledge/Ability (MP-05) scores are
recorded

Terminal State: Consolidated ADKAR/Bridges signal evaluated against the
Divergence Pattern check, confirmed aligned or escalated

E2E-ERP — ERP Implementation Lifecycle

  Status: Proposed extension

Detailed Description

The ERP Implementation lifecycle tracks the human side of a large-scale
enterprise system rollout — the most common transformation archetype in
the journi seed dataset (PRJ-001, PRJ-004, PRJ-007). Where the project
management track tracks technical configuration, data migration and
cutover, this E2E chain tracks the parallel people journey: stakeholder
impact assessment (MP-01) identifies which roles and sites are affected;
sponsorship and governance (MP-02) secures executive backing through the

hypercare (MP-09) provides floor-level coaching immediately
post-go-live; and sustainment (MP-10) confirms the new system is the
durable new normal. This is the canonical “ERP adoption” chain — every
ERP implementation is, at its heart, a people transformation as much as
a technical one.

Trigger: ERP project charter approved, Executive Sponsor confirmed

Terminal State: Production system live, all cohorts trained and capable,
hypercare complete, Refreeze confirmed from evidence

E2E-BPR — Business Process Reengineering Lifecycle

  Status: Proposed extension

Detailed Description

The Business Process Reengineering lifecycle tracks a clean-slate
redesign of an end-to-end business process — explicitly not an
incremental fix to the existing process. Where ERP implementation works
within an existing process framework, BPR starts from a blank page. The
human change challenge is correspondingly larger: roles, reporting lines
and even professional identities may be redesigned alongside the process
itself. Stakeholder impact assessment (MP-01) must explicitly bracket
“why it’s done this way” to avoid anchoring the redesign to the old
process. Sponsorship (MP-02) must sustain backing through the ambiguity
of clean-slate design. Communication (MP-03) must address role-change
anxiety early. Training (MP-05) builds capability against a
fundamentally new way of working. Divergence detection (MP-08) is
critical here — a cohort can score well on new-process Knowledge/Ability
while still grieving the old identity (the classic “compliance without
commitment” trap). Hypercare (MP-09) provides floor-level coaching
through the highest-disruption period, and sustainment (MP-10) confirms
the new process has genuinely replaced the old one. This chain
operationalizes the BPR-specific failure mode warning from the
Transformation Types Guide: “Redesign anchored to the old process” is
explicitly guarded against by MP-01’s clean-slate framing.

Trigger: Process redesign mandate approved, clean-slate approach
confirmed

Terminal State: New process rolled out across all sites, old process
formally retired, performance gains confirmed across multiple reporting
cycles

side of automating a defined workflow that orchestrates four kinds of

support through the transition from “doing” to “overseeing.” Sustainment
(MP-10) hands the automated assets to a permanent Center of Excellence.
This chain operationalizes the BPA-specific failure mode: “Stepping down
human review too early” is guarded against by MP-09’s evidence-gated
taper and MP-08’s divergence checks.

approved

  Status: Proposed extension

The Integrated Management System (QHSE) lifecycle tracks the human side
of designing and certifying a unified management system against
international standards (ISO 9001, ISO 45001, ISO 14001, etc.) —
integrating policy, process and controls into a single auditable system.
The human change challenge is distinctive: the final gate is an external
certification body’s pass/fail decision, not an internal go/no-go call.

provides support through the mock-up and certifying audits. Sustainment
(MP-10) hands the system to the standing quality function for the
ongoing surveillance-audit cycle. This chain operationalizes the
IMS-specific failure mode: “Documentation without daily practice” is
guarded against by MP-07’s observation-based signals and MP-08’s
divergence checks.

Trigger: Certification requirement confirmed, target standard(s)
identified

Terminal State: Certification achieved (or "pass with minor
nonconformities"), surveillance cycle active

E2E-CULT — Cultural / Values Transformation Lifecycle

  Status: Proposed extension

Detailed Description

The Cultural / Values Transformation lifecycle tracks a deliberate shift
in an organization’s shared values, norms and behaviors — how people
actually act, not merely what they are told. This is the longest and
most fragile transformation type in the journi catalogue (18-36 months).
The object of change is behavioral and identity-based rather than
procedural, so Bridges and Kübler-Ross carry proportionally more
analytical weight than ADKAR’s Knowledge/Ability blocks. Stakeholder
impact assessment (MP-01) must combine survey data with direct
behavioral observation — the gap between stated and lived values is
rarely visible from survey data alone. Sponsorship (MP-02) must model
the target behaviors visibly, not just endorse them. Communication
(MP-03) must frame the change as an invitation to participate, backed by
visible leadership modeling. Resistance management (MP-04) must address
skepticism about genuine commitment — “this is just corporate talk” is
the most common barrier reason. The champion network (MP-06) must extend
to the middle-manager layer, where the gap between senior rhetoric and
daily reality is most keenly felt. Readiness diagnostics (MP-07) must
track behavioral observation data alongside survey scores. Divergence
detection (MP-08) catches the pattern of people who have learned to say
the right things while still operating by the old norms. Hypercare
(MP-09) provides sustained coaching through the organization-wide
rollout. Sustainment (MP-10) must be deliberately provisional — culture
work never truly ends. This chain operationalizes the Culture-specific
failure mode: “Values as posters, not practice” is guarded against by
MP-01’s behavioral observation and MP-07’s observation-based signals.

Trigger: Culture gap diagnosed, target values and behaviors defined

Terminal State: Target behaviors embedded in leadership development and
HR practice, Refreeze confirmed provisionally with ongoing monitoring

E2E-OM — Operating Model Redesign Lifecycle

  Status: Proposed extension

Detailed Description

The Operating Model Redesign lifecycle tracks the human side of
redesigning how the organization is structured and coordinated to
deliver its strategy — org structure, governance, decision rights,
capabilities, locations and ways of working. This typically spans
multiple functions at once. The human change challenge is structural:
people’s roles, reporting lines and decision authority change, and
persistent cross-functional friction is often a symptom of unclear
decision rights rather than a personality conflict. Stakeholder impact
assessment (MP-01) must assess the current operating model across
structure, governance, decision rights, capabilities and locations.
Sponsorship (MP-02) must sustain backing through the detailed org
design. Communication (MP-03) must be honest about what is and isn’t yet
decided. Training (MP-05) must build capability in the new governance
forums and decision-rights model. Readiness diagnostics (MP-07) must
track whether decisions are actually flowing through the new structure,
not just whether the org chart has updated. Divergence detection (MP-08)
catches managers who operate the new structure competently while still
emotionally tied to the old one. Hypercare (MP-09) provides support
through the full organizational transition. Sustainment (MP-10) confirms
the new structure has survived at least one full planning or budget
cycle without reverting. This chain operationalizes the Operating
Model-specific failure mode: “Structure changes on paper, decisions
don’t change in practice” is guarded against by MP-07’s
governance-adoption tracking and MP-08’s divergence checks.

Trigger: Operating model redesign mandate approved, target operating
model (TOM) blueprint selected

Terminal State: New structure and governance operational, stable across
a full planning or budget cycle, Refreeze confirmed

monitoring to the permanent control owner. This chain operationalizes
the Compliance-specific failure mode: “Training completion mistaken for
compliance” is guarded against by MP-07’s control-operation tracking and
MP-09’s first live monitoring cycle.

deadline

E2E-TSD — Training & Skills Development Lifecycle

  Status: Proposed extension

Detailed Description

The Training & Skills Development lifecycle tracks capability-building
as the primary transformation vehicle — where the change itself is
fundamentally about equipping people with new skills, rather than
changing a system, process, structure or culture. This archetype is
distinctive because the “what” of the change (the new capability) is
also the primary mechanism of adoption: people become ready for the
change by acquiring the skills the change requires. Stakeholder impact
assessment (MP-01) must identify skills gaps and training needs by role.
Sponsorship (MP-02) must sustain backing through the training rollout.
Communication (MP-03) must frame the training as an opportunity, not a
burden. Training and capability enablement (MP-05) is the core of this
chain — curriculum design, delivery, competency verification and
remediation. The champion network (MP-06) extends peer support and
coaching. Readiness diagnostics (MP-07) tracks training completion and
competency verification — the Composite Readiness Index’s training
component carries extra weight. Divergence detection (MP-08) catches the
“trained but not capable” pattern. Hypercare (MP-09) provides on-the-job
coaching and reinforcement. Sustainment (MP-10) embeds the new skills
into performance management and ongoing development. This chain
operationalizes the Training-specific reality: “Trained” and “capable”
are not the same thing — Module 10 explicitly separates attendance from
demonstrated competency.

Trigger: Skills gap identified, new capability required, or training
mandate approved

Terminal State: Target population competent (verified by assessment),
performance sustained, training embedded in ongoing development
